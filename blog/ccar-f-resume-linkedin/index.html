How to List the CCAR-F (CCA-F) on a Resume and LinkedIn

By CCA Practice Platforms · September 21, 2026

You passed. Here is the short version of how to show it:

Claude Certified Architect – Foundations (CCAR-F), Anthropic. Earned September 2026, expires September 2027.

That line goes in a Certifications section on your resume and in Licenses & certifications on LinkedIn, with your own month in place of September. The rest of this article explains each part of it, how to make the credential do real work on your profile, and how to handle the expiry date the credential carries.

The exact wording

The exam guide names the credential "Claude Certified Architect – Foundations" and gives its exam code as CCAR-F (Section 3). You will also see it written CCA-F, and there is more on why the exam has two codes if a recruiter asks.

Anthropic itself writes the name two ways: the exam guide uses a dash, and Anthropic's announcement of its role-based certifications uses a colon, "Claude Certified Architect: Foundations". Either is recognizable. The rule that settles it is simple: match your badge. Anthropic's announcement says that people who pass receive a digital badge through Credly by Pearson, and its Academy FAQ says the credential is issued through Credly. Whatever your badge shows is the version to copy, character for character, everywhere you list it.

On a resume

A Certifications section is the standard home. Put the full name first, then the issuer, then the dates:

Claude Certified Architect – Foundations (CCAR-F) | Anthropic | Sep 2026 to Sep 2027

Whether it also belongs in your summary line at the top is a judgment call, and here is the test we would use. Section 2 of the guide describes the candidate the exam is built for: "a solution architect who designs and implements production applications with Claude". If that sentence describes work you actually do, the credential earns a place in your summary, because it confirms the claim your summary is already making. If you are earlier in your career, keep it in Certifications and let your experience section carry the story. That is our advice, not the guide's, and it rests on one idea: a credential is strongest when it confirms something the rest of the page already shows.

It also helps to know who the certification is built for, since the same description tells you which of your experiences to put next to it.

On LinkedIn

Add it under Licenses & certifications. The form asks for a name, an issuing organization, dates, and optionally a credential ID and URL.

  • Name: exactly as your Credly badge shows it.
  • Issuing organization: Anthropic.
  • Issue date: the month you were awarded the credential.
  • Expiration date: twelve months later (see the next section).
  • Credential ID and URL: fill these in only from your Credly badge. Neither Anthropic's announcement nor its Academy FAQ describes a separate ID or verification link for the exam credential, so copy whatever the badge provides and nothing else.

Your headline and About section follow the same test as a resume summary. If you design and build production systems with Claude, "Claude Certified Architect – Foundations" in the headline tells a reader what kind of work you do before they open your profile. If you are moving into that work, put the credential in your About section next to what you are building.

The expiry date

Section 15 is clear that this credential does not last forever: it "is valid for 12 months from the date it is awarded." The guide gives the reason directly: "Because the underlying technology evolves rapidly, the credential is time-limited so that holders maintain current knowledge."

List the expiry date. It is accurate, and it tells a reader the credential is current, which is the point of holding one. (There is more on how long the credential lasts in our FAQ.)

Renewal is straightforward. The guide says: "To renew on time, you review what has changed since you certified and complete a free, non-proctored assessment on the Anthropic Partner Academy. There is no fee for on-time renewal." When you renew, update the expiry date on your resume and on LinkedIn. It is also worth knowing how renewal compares with the Associate certification if you plan to hold more than one.

If a credential lapses, the guide says "you must retake the full exam at the full fee to regain certified status." On your profile, a lapsed credential keeps its real dates, with the end date in the past. Never leave it open-ended. An honest date range still shows the work you did to earn it.

Turning the domains into resume lines

The credential tells a reader you were examined on something. Your experience section tells them what you did with it. The guide's own structure is a good map for connecting the two: Section 4 lists five domains, and Section 2 lists the hands-on experience the exam assumes. You can see the five domains and their weights laid out on our exam guide page.

The patterns below are for describing your own work. Fill the brackets with what you actually built and what actually happened. Do not copy them as claims.

  • Agentic Architecture & Orchestration (27%): "Designed [a multi-agent or agentic workflow] using the Claude Agent SDK to [what it did], [a result you can point to]."
  • Claude Code Configuration & Workflows (20%): "Configured Claude Code for [team or project] with [CLAUDE.md files, Agent Skills, MCP integrations], [what changed for the team]."
  • Prompt Engineering & Structured Output (20%): "Built [an extraction or generation pipeline] producing structured output against [a JSON schema or format], [a reliability result you measured]."
  • Tool Design & MCP Integration (18%): "Designed MCP tools and resources connecting Claude to [backend system], [what it enabled]."
  • Context Management & Reliability (15%): "Handled [long documents, long conversations or agent handoffs] with [the approach you used], [what it fixed or prevented]."

One line per domain you have genuinely worked in is plenty. Two strong lines are worth more than five vague ones.

If you are still preparing

You can mention an exam you have not passed yet, as long as the wording is exact. If your exam is booked, "Claude Certified Architect – Foundations (CCAR-F): exam scheduled, [month]" is accurate. If it is not booked yet, leave it off and let your project work speak for itself. Never write "certified" before you are, and never use the badge name for a credential you do not hold.

In interviews

A credential on a resume invites the question "what was on it?" Here is how to answer well.

Talk about how the exam is designed, because that says something about what you can do. Section 1 says candidates must demonstrate "practical judgment about architecture, configuration, and tradeoffs in production deployments." Section 5 explains that the exam presents four scenarios, picked at random from six, each one "a realistic production context that frames a set of questions." So you can say, truthfully, that you were tested on making design decisions in production scenarios, then move straight to a decision you made in your own work.

Do not describe specific questions. Section 14 is the confidentiality agreement every candidate accepts, and Section 13 lists "revocation of your credential" among the consequences for disclosing exam content. The design of the exam is public; its content is not. The best interview answer uses the first and moves on to your own work.

What this article does not claim

The CCAR-F exam guide says nothing about salaries, hiring rates, or whether employers require or prefer the credential, and neither does this article. What the credential gives you is a precise statement that you were examined on production Claude architecture, with a Credly badge to show it and a date to show it is current. Listed exactly and paired with real work, that statement does its job.

FAQ

What is the exact name to put on a resume?

The exam guide calls it "Claude Certified Architect – Foundations", exam code CCAR-F. Anthropic also writes it with a colon. Copy the name exactly as it appears on your Credly badge.

Who is the issuing organization on LinkedIn?

Anthropic. Anthropic says people who pass receive a digital badge through Credly by Pearson, and that the credential is issued through Credly, so take any credential ID or URL from your Credly badge.

Should I list an expiration date?

Yes. Section 15 of the guide says the credential is valid for 12 months from the date it is awarded. Update the date when you renew.

Can I list the exam before I pass?

Only as what it is. "Exam scheduled" with a month is accurate if the exam is booked. Never write "certified" before you are.
